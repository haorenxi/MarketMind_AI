# Evidence standards

## Source priority

Prefer sources in roughly this order, adjusting for the claim:

1. Laws, regulators, government datasets, standards bodies, filings, audited reports, and original research papers.
2. Official company documentation, pricing, repositories, announcements, and investor materials.
3. Reputable research institutions and established industry publications with visible methodology.
4. Credible news reporting and expert analysis.
5. Reviews, forums, and social posts for experience or sentiment claims only.

Do not treat aggregation pages, unattributed summaries, or search snippets as strong evidence for material claims.

## Claim handling

- Record the claim, source URL, source type, relevant date, and any caveat.
- Use the event date rather than publication date when recency depends on when something happened.
- Cross-check consequential claims when practical, especially market size, rankings, adoption, performance, and competitive claims.
- Preserve incompatible estimates separately and explain differing definitions instead of averaging them.
- Separate absence of evidence from evidence of absence.

## Conflicts and gaps

When sources disagree, compare scope, definitions, dates, incentives, and methodology. State which source is more applicable and why. If the conflict cannot be resolved, report a range or competing claims and reduce confidence.

When evidence is unavailable, name the missing evidence and avoid filling the gap with an unsupported estimate.
