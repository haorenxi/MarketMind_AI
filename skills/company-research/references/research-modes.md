# Research modes

Read only the sections relevant to the request. Combine modes when the user's decision spans more than one.

## Company

Cover identity and ownership, business model, products, customers, geography, management, financial or operating signals, partnerships, recent events, and material risks. Separate company statements from independently verified facts.

## Competitor

Define the comparison set and basis before comparing. Examine positioning, target customers, product capabilities, pricing, distribution, traction, defensibility, and weaknesses. Avoid false precision when vendors disclose different metrics.

## Product

Cover customer problem, segments, core capabilities, pricing and packaging, adoption signals, reviews, alternatives, limitations, and use cases. Distinguish vendor documentation from user-reported experience.

## Market

Define geography, period, category boundary, and metric before stating market size. Compare estimates only when their definitions are compatible. Cover demand drivers, constraints, segments, growth signals, major participants, and scenario risks.

## Supply chain

Map upstream inputs, enabling technologies, midstream producers or integrators, downstream channels and customers, geographic concentration, bottlenecks, substitution options, and regulatory or geopolitical exposure.

## Technology and papers

Cover technical approaches, maturity, benchmarks, limitations, leading organizations, recent primary papers, reproducibility, and the gap between research results and commercial deployment. Prefer original papers and official repositories.

## Open-source ecosystem

Examine repository ownership, license, release activity, contributor distribution, issue and pull-request health, documentation, dependencies, adoption signals, governance, and maintenance risk. Stars are a popularity signal, not proof of production adoption.
